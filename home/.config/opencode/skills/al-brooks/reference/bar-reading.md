# Bar-by-bar Reading

Brooks reads every bar as a battle between buyers and sellers. The close
records the outcome. A bar means nothing alone; its meaning comes from where
it sits in the structure. This file catalogs the bar types and patterns he
reads, and the rules for classifying them.

## Anatomy of a bar

- **Body** - the range between open and close. Who controlled the bar.
- **Upper/lower shadow** - rejected prices; how far the losers pushed back.
- **Close** - the single most important fact. The close decides who won.
- **Range** - the distance high to low. Big range = strong emotion.

The three questions for every bar:
1. Did it close in the direction of the current trend or against it?
2. Is its range large (a trend bar) or small (a pullback/doji)?
3. Does it close near its high, near its low, or in the middle?

## Bar types

| Type | Definition | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Bull trend bar** | Closes near its high; body is a large fraction of the range | Buyers controlled the bar. Strong in a bull trend, suspect at a range high. |
| **Bear trend bar** | Closes near its low; body is a large fraction of the range | Sellers controlled the bar. Strong in a bear trend, suspect at a range low. |
| **Pullback bar** | Small range, closes against the current trend direction | A pause in the trend; often the entry trigger for a pullback setup. |
| **Doji** | Body is very small relative to range (open ~ close) | Complete indecision; the battle ended in a draw. Strongly context-dependent. |
| **Inside bar** | Entire range inside the prior bar's range | Compression; a calm bar that often precedes a breakout. |
| **Outside bar** | Range engulfs the prior bar's range | A burst of emotion; can mark a breakout or an exhaustion reversal. |
| **Reversal bar** | Strong close against the prior swing direction, at a key level | Buyers (bull reversal bar) or sellers (bear reversal bar) seized control. |
| **Signal bar** | The bar immediately before an entry; a strong trend bar in the entry direction | The trigger. The setup is not valid without a credible signal bar. |
| **Neutral bar** | No clear character; mid body, mid range | Low information; wait for a trend bar to act. |

## Bar patterns

- **Bar pair** - two consecutive bars read as one unit (e.g. a big bar
  followed by a small pullback). Pairs are the smallest structure with meaning.
- **ii pattern (inside inside)** - two consecutive inside bars. Strong
  compression; the following breakout bar is often a good entry.
- **Two-legged pullback** - a pullback that has two pushes against the trend
  (e.g. a small low, a lower low). Common in trends and often the end of a
  pullback.
- **Climax bar** - an unusually large trend bar, typically after a long move.
  Marks emotional exhaustion; often the last push before a pullback or
  reversal.

## Trend bars and context

The same bar type flips meaning with location:

- A **bull trend bar at a range low** after a pullback = buyers returning
  (buy setup).
- A **bull trend bar at a range high** = buyers exhausting; often the start
  of a reversal (sell the strength).
- A **bear trend bar at a range high** = sellers controlling (sell setup).
- A **bear trend bar at a range low** = sellers exhausting; watch for a
  reversal.

Rule of thumb: **a strong trend bar that closes beyond a key level (swing
point, measured move, range extreme) is meaningful. The same bar inside the
range is noise.**

## Signal bars

A valid signal bar (the trigger for an entry) must:

1. Be a **strong trend bar** in the entry direction (large range, close near
   its extreme).
2. Be at a **reasonable location** (pullback, breakout, or key level) - not
   mid-range.
3. Ideally be preceded by a pause (pullback bar or doji) so the market is not
   already extended.

A doji or a weak bar is never a signal bar. If there is no signal bar, there
is no clean setup.

## Classifying a bar (rule sequence)

1. Record close, body size, range size, close position within the range.
2. Classify the bar type from the anatomy (trend bar, pullback, doji,
   inside/outside, reversal, neutral).
3. Determine context: phase (from market-structure.md), swing location, and
   whether it closes beyond any key level.
4. Decide meaning from type + context, and whether it can be a signal bar for
   any setup.
